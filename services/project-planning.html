Project planning and scheduling for construction and infrastructure

A programme that reflects how the work will actually be built, kept current from award to completion.

Talk to us

A programme is only useful if the people running the job believe it. That means it is built from the method and the resources, not from the dates someone hopes for, and it is kept current so it still tells the truth in month nine.

Planned plans and schedules construction and infrastructure work for clients, contractors and consultancies. We build the programme, take it through acceptance, keep it updated through delivery, and recover it when it slips. For work that is specifically about the Primavera P6 tool, such as health checks, recovering an inherited file or setting up P6 itself, see our Primavera P6 consultant page.

Where we come in

  • At bid: a tender programme built to the ITT, scored by the evaluator and ready to become the contract programme.
  • After award: the first programme for acceptance, with the content the contract requires.
  • During delivery: fortnightly or monthly updates, a narrative on what moved and why, and early warning while there is still time to act.
  • When it slips: a recovery programme the team can actually work to, with the options and their cost in time set out plainly.
  • When it is disputed: the programme records and analysis behind delay and claims work.

What a programme we build has

  • A work breakdown that follows how the job will be built and reported, agreed with the people running it.
  • Logic that drives the dates: every activity tied to what it depends on, no constraints doing the work of missing links.
  • Durations derived from quantities, gang sizes and output rates where those are known, and stated as assumptions where they are not.
  • Calendars, access dates, possessions or outages, and the client's key dates built in, not added afterwards.
  • Time risk allowances shown where the contract expects them, not hidden in long durations.
  • A short narrative that explains sequence, critical path and the main assumptions in plain English.

How we build one

  1. Read the contract, the scope and the client's programme requirements before opening the software.
  2. Agree the structure and the level of detail with the project team.
  3. Build the logic and durations with the people who will do the work, not from a template.
  4. Load resources and cost where the project needs them for levelling, earned value or reporting.
  5. Check the critical path, float and logic quality against the DCMA 14 point checks and the client's own thresholds.
  6. Issue the baseline with its narrative, then agree how it will be updated.

Tools

Primavera P6 and Oracle Primavera Cloud are our main platforms, and most clients in our sectors specify one of them. Where a client requires another tool, we work in it. We hold our own P6 licences, so you do not need one, and we issue native files and PDF in the format your client asks for.

Who does the work

A named lead from our planning team runs your programme, and a senior planner reviews every issue before it leaves us. When the work needs more capacity, we draw on our bench of senior planners and specialists who work to the same standards. See how we work.

Common Questions

What is the difference between this and your Primavera P6 consultant service?

Project planning covers the programme itself: building it, keeping it current and recovering it. Our P6 consultant service covers the software, such as health checks, recovering an inherited P6 file and configuring P6.

Can you take over a programme someone else built?

Yes. We check it first, tell you what can be kept and what needs rebuilding, and then take it forward from the next update.

How often do you update a programme?

At the frequency the contract or the client requires, usually fortnightly or monthly, with a narrative on what moved and why.

Do you work under NEC, JCT and FIDIC?

Yes. Most UK work is under NEC4 or JCT, and Gulf work is under FIDIC. The programme is built to what the contract requires it to show.

Do we need our own P6 licence?

No. We work in our own licensed Primavera P6 and issue native files and PDF.
