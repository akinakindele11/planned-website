Project controls and PMO support

Progress, cost, change and reporting tied to the programme, so the numbers the board sees are the numbers on site.

Talk to us

Project controls are what turn a programme into management information: how much work is done, what it has cost, what has changed, and what that means for the end date and the final cost. When controls are weak, the monthly report says one thing and the site says another, and the gap is usually found too late.

Planned sets up and runs project controls around a Primavera P6 programme for clients, contractors and programme teams in construction, energy, transport and utilities. We work inside your systems where you have them, and set them up where you do not.

What we set up and run

  • Progress measurement: rules for measuring progress that the site can apply consistently, and updates recorded against site evidence rather than estimates.
  • Earned value: a cost or resource-loaded baseline, planned value, earned value and actual cost, with performance indices and forecasts at completion that can be traced back to the programme.
  • Change control: a change and early warning register tied to the programme, so the time and cost effect of each change is assessed and recorded, including compensation events under NEC.
  • Risk: a risk register linked to the activities it affects, with quantitative schedule risk analysis where the project needs confidence levels.
  • Reporting: a monthly report and dashboards, including Power BI where you use it, built from the programme and cost data rather than retyped.

PMO support

On larger programmes the controls problem is consistency: many projects, many planners and many suppliers reporting in different ways. We set the standards (structure, coding, calendars, progress rules, reporting layouts), write the procedures, and run supplier programme governance, including review of contractor programmes against NEC clauses 31 and 32. Our case studies include this work for transmission, gas distribution and highways programmes.

Starting mid-project

Most controls work does not start on day one. We establish where the project actually is, rebuild a credible baseline or a current forecast from the records, agree what will be measured from here, and put the monthly cycle in place. The first report then gives an honest starting position rather than inheriting old optimism.

How we work

A named lead from our controls team runs the work, and a senior planner reviews every report before issue. We document the procedures and, where you want it, train your team to run the cycle themselves. See how we work.

Common Questions

Do you replace our controls team or work with it?

Either. We can run the controls cycle for you, or work alongside your team to set it up and hand it over.

Can earned value be added to a programme that is already running?

Yes, if the programme can be resource or cost loaded. We set a baseline from the current position and measure from there, and say plainly what the early months can and cannot show.

Which tools do you use?

Primavera P6 or Oracle Primavera Cloud for the programme, your cost system for actuals, and Excel or Power BI for reporting, depending on what you already use.

Do you review contractor or supplier programmes?

Yes. We review programmes against the contract, including NEC clause 31 acceptance and clause 32 revisions, and report what complies and what does not.

How often is the controls cycle run?

Usually monthly, with fortnightly or weekly progress on fast-moving work. We agree the cycle with you at the start.

Need controls set up or run?

Tell us the project, its stage and what reporting is expected. We reply within one working day.
