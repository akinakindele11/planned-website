Delay analysis and extension of time

What the delay was, what caused it and what time it is worth, set out so it holds up when it is tested.

Talk to us

Time claims are usually won or lost on records rather than argument. A party with a sound entitlement and poor programme records will lose to one with a weaker case and better evidence. The work is to establish what actually happened, show the effect on completion, and present it in a way the other side, an adjudicator or a tribunal can follow.

Planned carries out delay and time analysis for contractors, clients and their advisers on construction and infrastructure projects under NEC, JCT and FIDIC, from a single compensation event to a full extension of time claim or defence.

What we do

  • Compensation event time assessment under NEC: the effect of an event on the Accepted Programme, impacted where it happened and linked to the work it affected, ready to support a quotation.
  • Extension of time claims: analysis of the delays, their causes and their effect on completion, with the narrative and programme evidence a JCT or FIDIC claim needs.
  • Responding to claims: for clients and their project managers, a review of a contractor's time claim or quotation, setting out what the evidence supports and what it does not.
  • Programme records: putting the as-built record, the updates and the narrative into a state that can support a claim, before one is needed.
  • Support to advisers: programme analysis and explanation for commercial teams and legal advisers preparing for negotiation or adjudication.

Choosing the method

The right method depends on the contract, the question being asked and the records available: impacted as-planned, time impact analysis, windows analysis, as-planned versus as-built or collapsed as-built. We say which method fits and why, and what it can and cannot show, before the analysis starts. Our guides on delay analysis methods and on why records matter more than method explain the reasoning.

Where to start

If you are not yet sure the position is worth pursuing, start with a Delay and EOT Position Review: a fixed scope, independent assessment of what the records support, which method fits and whether a full analysis is worth commissioning.

Who does the work

A named lead from our planning team carries out the analysis, and a senior planner reviews it before issue. Where the work is for a dispute, we say plainly what the evidence supports, including where that is unwelcome. See how we work.

Common Questions

Do you act for contractors or clients?

Both. We assess delay for contractors claiming time and review claims for clients and project managers. Where an existing relationship would compromise our independence, we say so and decline.

Which contracts do you work under?

NEC4 and NEC3, JCT and FIDIC. The analysis follows what the contract says about time, programmes and notices.

Can you assess a single compensation event?

Yes. We assess the time effect of individual compensation events on the Accepted Programme to support a quotation.

What if our programme records are poor?

We tell you what can still be shown and what cannot. A Delay and EOT Position Review is designed to answer that before you spend on a full analysis.

Which delay analysis method do you use?

The one that fits the contract, the question and the records. We explain the choice and its limits before starting.
