MS Project to Primavera P6 Conversion

We rebuild your MS Project programme in Primavera P6, to your main contractor's specification, and get it accepted

Get Started Today

If your main contractor or client now requires the construction programme in Primavera P6, converting it is not a File, Export. The logic, calendars, work breakdown, constraints and critical path all have to survive the move, and then the programme has to meet the client's P6 requirements before it will be accepted. We do both.

What we do

  • Rebuild your MS Project programme in Primavera P6, to your main contractor's or client's specification.
  • Preserve and correct the logic, calendars, WBS, constraints and critical path, so the schedule still means what it should.
  • Set the P6 project settings, activity codes and calendars the specification asks for.
  • Deliver the native files the client wants, the .xer and, where required, .xml and .plf, with a PDF and a short programme narrative.
  • Support the client's review and programme workshop, and provide a revision once their comments come back.

Why it is more than a conversion

Clients rarely just want the same programme in P6. They issue a P6 specification. A typical one requires physical percent complete, fixed duration and units, retained logic, the critical path shown as the longest path in red, the client's own activity coding, finish to start logic, short activity durations, no open ends and no constraints, plus S curves, resource histograms and man hours. A straight export from MS Project fails most of these. We rebuild the programme so it complies, rather than exporting something that will be rejected.

What you get

  • A Primavera P6 baseline built to the client's specification and ready for acceptance.
  • The native .xer file, with .xml and .plf where required, a PDF, and a programme narrative.
  • The critical path, S curves and resource histograms the specification calls for.
  • A programme that is ready for the client's programme workshop, not one that bounces back with comments.

Independent, by design

We plan, we do not build. Our only interest is whether the programme is true and whether it will be accepted, which is exactly what a main contractor's planner is checking. That independence is the point.

Keep it updated

Most P6 specifications also require weekly and monthly updates once the baseline is approved, usually a native file and PDF submitted by a set time each week. We run those updates for you, to the specification, and we can host the programme in our own licensed Primavera P6, so you do not need a licence of your own. If your programme was built in MS Project and now needs to move, our guide on rebuilding a programme to a main contractor's P6 specification walks through what acceptance actually takes.
