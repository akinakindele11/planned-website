Schedule risk analysis (QSRA)

How confident you can be in the completion date, what is driving the risk, and how much time to hold.

Talk to us

A deterministic programme gives one date. Schedule risk analysis asks how likely that date is, given the uncertainty in durations and the risks that could hit the work. The answer is a range of dates with a confidence level attached, the risks that drive it, and the contingency needed to reach the confidence your client or board expects.

Planned carries out quantitative schedule risk analysis (QSRA) on Primavera P6 programmes for clients and contractors in construction, energy, transport and utilities, at business case, tender, baseline and recovery stages.

When it is usually asked for

  • At a business case or stage gate, where funding depends on a date stated at a given confidence level.
  • At tender, to set realistic key dates and price the time risk.
  • When a baseline is set, to size the time risk allowance and show where it should sit.
  • When a project is slipping, to test whether a recovery plan is credible.

How we do it

  1. Check the programme first. Simulation run on poor logic gives confident-looking nonsense. We check logic, constraints and the critical path, and fix or flag what would distort the result.
  2. Agree the uncertainty. Three-point duration ranges, set with the people who know the work, for the activities that matter.
  3. Map the risks. Risks from your register, with probability and impact, mapped to the activities they would affect.
  4. Run the simulation. Monte Carlo analysis of the programme, with correlation set so the result is not falsely optimistic.
  5. Explain the result. Confidence levels on completion and key milestones, the activities and risks driving them, and what mitigation would be worth.

What you get

  • Completion and key milestone dates at the confidence levels you need, with the distribution behind them.
  • Sensitivity analysis showing which risks and activities drive the result.
  • A recommended schedule contingency and where it should sit in the programme.
  • A short report written for the people making the decision, not only for planners.

Who does the work

A named lead from our planning team runs the analysis and the workshops, and a senior planner reviews the model and the report before issue. See how we work.

Common Questions

What is a P50 or P80 date?

The date by which the simulation shows the work finishing in 50 or 80 per cent of the runs. Many infrastructure clients and funders ask for key dates at P80.

Can you run QSRA on our existing programme?

Yes. We check it first, because the result is only as good as the logic. Where the programme needs work, we tell you before the analysis, not after.

Do you run the risk workshops?

Yes. We facilitate the workshops to agree duration ranges and map the risks to activities, with the people who know the work.

Is this the same as a risk register?

No. A register lists risks. QSRA tests what those risks and the uncertainty in durations do to the dates, and how much time to hold against them.

How long does an analysis take?

It depends on the size and state of the programme and the risk register. We confirm the timescale when we scope the work.
