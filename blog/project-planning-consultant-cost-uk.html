There is no single price for a project planning consultant. What you pay depends on what you need done, how large and complicated the schedule is, the contract and assurance standard it has to meet, how quickly you need it, and whether you are buying a person, a defined deliverable or ongoing capacity. This guide sets out what moves the fee and how to brief consultants so the quotes you get back can be compared fairly.

Fees depend on scope; we confirm them in writing before work starts. Our working terms are published on How We Work.

Is this the "planning consultant" you're looking for?

If you landed here hoping to price up help with a planning application or planning permission, you want a town planning consultant, not us. That's a different profession entirely: advising on land use, listed buildings and local authority applications.

This guide is about project planning and scheduling consultants: the people who build, maintain and interrogate your Primavera P6, Oracle Primavera Cloud or MS Project programme, and the wider project controls discipline that sits around it (cost control, earned value management, schedule risk analysis). If that's you, read on.

Three ways the work is bought

The engagement model often matters more than the headline rate, because it decides who carries the risk of the work taking longer than expected.

Time and materials (day rate). Best for ongoing, hard-to-scope work, such as an embedded planner supporting a live programme. Cost is transparent but open-ended.

Fixed fee. Best for a defined deliverable: build and baseline a new programme, run a one-off DCMA 14-point health check, produce a QSRA report ahead of a board decision. You know the total before work starts, and the consultancy carries the estimating risk.

Retainer. Best where you need guaranteed capacity, such as a fractional project controls function, without carrying a full-time headcount. Usually agreed as a committed number of days each month.

Before you compare quotes, it helps to know what state your own schedule is in. A well-built programme is quicker for any consultant to pick up than one that has drifted. Our Free Schedule Health Check gives you that read in a few minutes.

What actually drives the fee?

Five things move a quote more than job title does.

1. Schedule complexity. Activity count, WBS depth, number of interfaces and calendars. A small subcontractor programme and a large nuclear decommissioning schedule are not the same job, even if both are "Primavera P6".

2. Contract requirements. NEC3 and NEC4 contracts, for instance, require an accepted programme submitted and updated to a defined content and frequency. That recurring obligation is itself a cost driver, separate from the underlying planning work.

3. Standards and assurance expected. A DCMA 14-point assessment, an APM-aligned maturity review, or formal earned value reporting all add scope beyond simply moving activity bars around.

4. Urgency and continuity. Bringing a consultant in to rescue a schedule mid-crisis, or to turn round a tender programme in days, takes more effort than planning the engagement well ahead.

5. IR35 status. For UK contract engagements, whether the role sits inside or outside IR35 changes the effective cost even at an identical quoted day rate. An inside-IR35 engagement carries employer's National Insurance and payroll costs on top of the rate, which is easy to miss when comparing a contractor with a consultancy's fixed fee.

Seniority: match it to the task

Statusing progress and producing routine reports does not need the same person as building a baseline, defending a programme at acceptance, running a schedule risk analysis or setting up earned value across a programme. Ask each supplier who will actually do the work, and who will check it, and make sure the seniority you are paying for matches the task.

In-house planner vs consultancy: the quick version

A permanent in-house planner carries fixed cost year-round, recruitment risk, and a single point of failure if they're on leave, or leave the business. A consultancy flexes with demand and brings exposure across many programmes, which matters most when you need DCMA-standard rigour, QSRA, or EVM capability you don't use often enough to justify a permanent hire. For most organisations the honest answer is "both, at different points in the project lifecycle."

How to get a quote you can actually compare

Before you ask three consultancies for a number, get clear on:

  • How many activities, calendars and resources are actually in (or planned for) the schedule.
  • Which standard you need to meet: DCMA 14-point, an APM competency framework, a specific NEC clause, or your own PMO governance.
  • Whether you need a person, a deliverable, or ongoing capacity.
  • What "done" looks like, so a fixed-fee quote is actually comparable across bidders.
  • Your deadline, and any dates that cannot move.

A vague brief gets a vague quote. A specific one gets a number you can hold a supplier to.

It's also worth asking directly whether the quote is inside or outside IR35, and whether it's a day rate or a fixed fee against the deliverable above. The two are not directly comparable without that context, and a supplier who can't answer either question quickly is worth a second look.

Common questions

How is a project planning consultant priced?

Usually as a day rate for open-ended support, a fixed fee for a defined deliverable, or a retainer for guaranteed capacity. Fees depend on scope; Planned confirms them in writing before work starts.

Is a day rate or a fixed fee better value?

It depends on the work. A day rate suits open-ended, hard-to-scope support. A fixed fee usually suits a defined deliverable such as a baseline build, a DCMA health check or a QSRA report, because the consultancy carries the estimating risk and you know the total before it starts.

Do we need our own Primavera P6 licence?

Not if the consultant hosts the programme in their own licensed P6. Planned builds in its own licensed P6 and issues native files and PDF, so you need no licence for the work we do. Oracle licence costs are separate from any consultancy fee.
